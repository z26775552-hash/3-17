The Reset

Aanya finally tells Nyla what 3:17 really is. But the truth has been waiting behind another door.

THE NIGHT HAS HAPPENED BEFORE
3:21 AM · THE HIDDEN ROOM

The closet door opened by itself.

Nyla backed away.

Nothing stood inside.

Only darkness.

Then something inside the darkness whispered:

“Aanya.”

Nyla's phone rang.

Aanya.

AANYA

“Do not answer it.”

The phone kept ringing.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Then the caller name changed.

NYLA

Nyla answered.

NYLA · PHONE

“You shouldn't have opened the closet.”

She dropped the phone.

From inside the closet, something laughed.

Aanya's voice came through the bedroom door.

AANYA

“Nyla. Open the door.”

Nyla stared at the handle.

“You're already inside,” she whispered.

Silence.

Then Aanya said:

“Not this time.”

The hallway lights went out.

Every light in the apartment went out with them.

In the darkness, Nyla heard someone walking behind her.

Not toward her.

Away from her.

Into the bedroom.

She turned.

The wardrobe was six inches away from the wall.

She had never moved it.

She knew that.

She also knew what was behind it.

That was the part that terrified her.

Nyla pushed the wardrobe aside.

A narrow door waited behind it.

Painted on the door:

317

Underneath:

DO NOT KNOCK.

Nyla reached for the handle.

Nyla opened the door.

A hallway stretched into darkness.

It was far longer than her apartment.

Doors lined both sides.

Every door had the same number.

3:17.

Some doors were scratched from the inside.

One was wet.

One had fingerprints pressed into the paint.

Small fingerprints.

Child-sized.

At the far end stood Aanya.

She looked exhausted.

Older than she should have been.

“You finally found it,” she said.

NYLA

“Found what?”

AANYA

“The place where the night keeps the people it doesn't know what to do with.”

Nyla looked at the doors.

Behind one of them, someone began crying.

Behind another, someone whispered her name.

Behind a third, there was knocking.

Three slow knocks.

Then a voice said:

“Nyla, please.”

Aanya grabbed her wrist.

AANYA

“Don't answer any door.”

“Why?”

Aanya looked at her.

“Because I did.”

She opened her hand.

In her palm was a photograph.

Three girls sat on a library floor.

Nyla.

Aanya.

And a third girl.

The third girl's face had been scratched away so violently the paper had torn.

On the back, in Nyla's handwriting:

SHE WAS HERE FIRST.

Nyla felt a memory push against the inside of her skull.

A red backpack.

Cheap headphones.

A girl laughing because Nyla had spilled coffee on her notes.

A name.

Almost.

Almost.

Then the hallway lights flickered.

Every door opened one inch.

Every voice stopped.

And from every room, in perfect unison:

“Remember me.”

Nyla's phone displayed one new message.
